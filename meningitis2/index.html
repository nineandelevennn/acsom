Slide "Big Picture" Core Answers

Addressing all high-yield board questions from the slide layout

📋

1. Where is the problem in meningitis?

Inflammation of the leptomeninges (arachnoid mater and pia mater) and the intervening subarachnoid space filled with CSF.

2. Where is CSF produced & absorbed?

Produced by ependymal cells of the Choroid Plexus inside brain ventricles; absorbed into venous circulation via Arachnoid Granulations into dural venous sinuses.

3. Bacterial Meningitis by Age Breakdown

  • Neonates (<1 month): Group B Strep, E. coli, Listeria.
  • Children/Teens (1m - 18y): N. meningitidis, S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae type b.
  • Adults (18 - 50y): S. pneumoniae, N. meningitidis.
  • Elderly (>50y / Immunocompromised): S. pneumoniae, Gram-neg bacilli, Listeria.

4. Which comes first: Head CT or LP?

Lumbar Puncture first EXCEPT if focal neurological deficit, papilledema, new-onset seizures, or altered sensorium → perform Head CT first to rule out herniation risk, but start empiric antibiotics BEFORE CT!

5. When is Dexamethasone used and why?

Administered prior to or concurrently with first dose of antibiotics in suspected S. pneumoniae (and H. influenzae) to attenuate inflammatory response caused by antibiotic-induced bacterial lysis, reducing sensorineural hearing loss and mortality.

6. Why headache, neck stiffness & photophobia?

Meningeal Irritation Triad: Inflammation stretches trigeminal/cervical nerve roots → Nuchal rigidity, Kernig (+), Brudzinski (+), and photophobia (trigeminal-optic reflex sensitivity).

7. Why Low Glucose & High Protein in Bacterial Meningitis?

Low Glucose (<40% blood glucose): Bacteria AND high density of infiltrating PMNs consume glucose + impaired hexose transport across blood-CSF barrier.
High Protein (>100 mg/dL): Massive breakdown of blood-brain barrier tight junctions allows serum proteins into CSF.

8. Standard Empirical Pharmacological Regimens

  • Neonates (<1 mo): Ampicillin + Cefotaxime (or Gentamicin). Avoid Ceftriaxone due to biliary sludging & kernicterus!
  • Age 1 mo - 50 yrs: Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone (or Cefotaxime).
  • Age > 50 yrs / Immunocompromised: Vancomycin + Ceftriaxone + Ampicillin (for Listeria coverage).

9. What is Waterhouse-Friderichsen Syndrome?

Fulminant meningococcemia (N. meningitidis) leading to severe DIC, endotoxin shock, petechial/purpuric skin lesions, and bilateral adrenal hemorrhage causing acute adrenal insufficiency.
